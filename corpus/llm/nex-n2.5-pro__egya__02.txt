When the first rain combs the red road behind our mother’s hut,

we gather broken calabashes, old photographs,

the rusted hinge of a school desk where the colonial map

once taught our fingers to fear their own borders,

and we set them beneath the iroko whose roots,

older than the census and the courthouse,

remember the feet that fled at dusk

with salt in their mouths and cassava leaves tied to their backs.

Nne, light the lamp.

Let the smoke climb like a question no soldier answered.

Let the cock, though hungry, announce the season

not for empire, not for the ledger of taxes,

but for the living who still bend to touch the soil

and ask, in whispers, whether
