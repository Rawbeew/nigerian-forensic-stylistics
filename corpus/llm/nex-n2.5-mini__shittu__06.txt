The value of craft is not hidden in speed, but in the discipline of return. To make something by hand is to accept that the world will not yield its meaning in one stroke; it must be approached, misshandled, revised, and approached again. In this sense, artistic labor is a form of knowledge. It is not mere production, not simply the manufacture of objects for display, but a patient negotiation between memory and material, between the inherited voice and the voice that must speak now.
