At dusk, when the baobab lets go of the last light and the red dust remembers every foot that crossed it, I sit with the silence as one sits with a child fevered by hunger, pressing cool cloth to its forehead.

We did not speak of the war, not properly, not in the clean words the dead deserve, because the dead do not drink tea from white cups, and because our mouths had been stitched by fear, by roadblocks, by mothers who watched the sky for bodies and found only locusts, only ash, only the black rain that fell when the rains themselves had learned to kill.
