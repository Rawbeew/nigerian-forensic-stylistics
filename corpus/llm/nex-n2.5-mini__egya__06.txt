When the first light comes over the wet fields, and the heron stands in the Ogun like a witness who has forgotten how to speak, we begin to understand that craft is not merely the shaping of clay or the arranging of words, but the patient obedience of the hand to whatever sorrow has entered the body, whether it comes from the heat of the city, the salt of the Niger Delta, or the old silence that survived the war and learned to move quietly through our villages.
