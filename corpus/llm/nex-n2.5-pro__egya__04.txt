When the elder sits beneath the ukwa tree,  
his voice, which the harmattan has thinned to a thread,  
draws the children close—those small drums of hunger and school uniforms—  
and he tells them how the river once carried names before it carried plastic,  
how the road, that bright wound cut by men with maps in their pockets,  
learned the taste of sandals, boots, bicycles, soldiers, and tax collectors.

Nna anyị, he says, do not trust a story that enters through only one ear.
